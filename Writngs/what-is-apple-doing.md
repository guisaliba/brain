## They refuse to even name it
For the last 2.5 years, Apple's been silent when it comes to AI. That made people think they were just sleeping on it, and lead everyone to two explanations for that: they either don't have a model and accepted they've lost the AI race; or they truly believe their AI features don't need to be good.

I'm sticking with the latter, but not exactly that. To me, it's not that Apple believes their AI features don't have to be good. They believe the hardware needs to be good.
The extraordinary AI features will only come true if the hardware can support it.

Because of that, they've been quietly sticking to their craft: making hardware. They've even refused to say the word "AI" during last year's WWDC [insert reference for that].
That's not sleeping on something. That's strategy.

But let me be honest with you: even though I said before that I don't stick with the "Apple don't have a model and just accepted it", I do actually believe that.

## Components are just components
Apple is not an AI lab, they're not building LLMs. That's not a guess, that's the truth.

That is so true that they've partnered with Google to bring Gemini to Siri [insert reference] and enhance it's capabilities of an AI assistant.

I think they did that not only to calm down investors complaining of Apple having no AI to offer while the rest of the  world is selling AI; but to prove their own hypothesis.

See, when they partner up with Google to put a bit of AI into the iPhone, one might think they're accepting a defeat. I think they're saying "hey, lend us a hand and help us see what your model is capable of doing in our phone".

Of course neither Google or any other company Apple chose to partner up with would refuse that. It's not any phone, it's the iPhone. To be able to advertise your AI as "the AI that powers the iPhone's assistant" speaks for itself.

Still, Google just won a supplier contract. Samsung makes the screens for the iPhone. Samsung is one of Apple's biggest competitors in phones. Still, no one says Samsung won the phone race because they make the screens.

No one's saying Google is winning the AI race too.

"When every part is good enough, a part is just a part. Components don't own the customers. The device does."

If models can't decide this race, something else will (hardware). Every time an AI model improves, the iPhone becomes more useful (and the MacBook, or any other product they sell might also).

Apple doesn't spend a dime for that. They just build what people use, and they keep crafting it for people to stick with it. This is what I think will enable them to not race for AI and still benefit the most off it.

## But what if AI companies entered the device fight?
Apple could measure that threat with a single question: "Can one of these companies build a hardware good enough to pull people off of the iPhone?".

I think OpenAI will try to pick that fight. Not with a phone to compete directly with the iPhone. Not a laptop to compete with the MacBook. OpenAI has reportedly been building an earphone [insert reference].

Two years ago OpenAI hired Jony Ive, the designer of Apple's most infamous devices: iPod, iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch... Know what else? The AirPods. Ive also designed them.

 OpenAI is probably going for an AI AirPods competitor, according to the rumours [insert reference for the earphone prototype OpenAI is building].

And I also think they might go after a home assistant [insert reference if found], hunting for Amazon's Alexa. GPT-6 Astra's announcement [insert video reference] strongly hints for that.

But will that be enough to make people switch from their AirPods, iPhones and Alexas? I don't think so.

## History repeats itself
1990s, Sega vs. Nintendo are fighting a videogame console war, just like OpenAI vs Anthropic and every other company selling AI.

They were trading one point leads because they couldn't hold it. By the end of the decade, Sega and Nintendo would have traded leads a dozen of times. Just like every new AI model release.

None of them won. Sony entered the game with the PlayStation and wiped the competition. But the PlayStation was indeed a generational leap. No one in AI seems to be getting to hold such a leap.

Not only that. The PlayStation was exclusive. You had to buy a PlayStation to get what it offered. Does that mean OpenAI would be willing to pull ChatGPT off of iPhone? Well, only if they have a product like Sony had PlayStation.

And they currently don't. So they wouldn't  bet everything on a hardware they've never shipped.

## What about Anthropic?
Anthropic is going the other way around. I don't think they're entering any fight other than the one they're already locked in: the models fight.

For now, Claude is the right product they need. Their goal is to make Claude the best LLM out there. They even think they're the only ones that can prevent AI bringing humanity into the brink of existence [insert reference on that].

I think they are going for that generational leap with Claude, for sure. And I wouldn't be surprised if they actually achieved it. I also wouldn't be surprised if they didn't.

## The endgame
Apple isn't safe though. If the models are good enough, then AI turns into a component. Components flow through whoever owns the customer. They're not untouchable, see?

I wouldn't say they're being threatened. I don't think they are. If this AI race were to be put into a cause and effect way, I'd say Apple is always the consequence.

They have been silently holding the endgame. They silently held their positions while everyone was too busy looking at NVIDIA and these frontier labs.

Tim Cook chose to not make Apple an AI driven company. They were never something else but a hardware company. 

And when he realized he made the right choice, he stepped away, for a hardware engineer [insert reference of Ternus becoming Apple's CEO earlier this year] to take his chair.

So, what is Apple doing? I think Apple is winning the race. Without even racing it.
